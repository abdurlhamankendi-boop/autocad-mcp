# Villa — materials and detail specification

Four materials, one grid. That is the whole strategy, and the discipline of holding
to it is what reads as expensive — not the cost of any individual item.

| | Material | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Honed travertine, unfilled, 40 mm | Ground plane inside and out, one continuous level |
| **2** | Board-marked in-situ concrete | Every structural wall left exposed; the stair core |
| **3** | Fumed European oak | All joinery, all doors, the stair treads |
| **4** | Bronze-anodised aluminium | Every window, every screen, every ironmonger's item |

Everything else — plaster, glass, water — is neutral. If a fifth material is proposed
on site, it is a mistake unless it displaces one of these four.

---

## 1. The setting-out grid

**One grid, 300 mm.** Stone joint, ceiling joint, door head, window head, skirting
line and shadow gap all land on it. Nothing is cut to fit; the grid moves instead.

- Door head, window head and the top of every full-height joinery run:
  **+2,700** from finished floor, on all floors.
- Ceiling at ground **+3,600**; at first **+3,200**; double-height void **+6,400**.
- Stone module **600 × 300**, running bond, joints **2 mm dry**.
- **No skirting.** A 15 mm recessed shadow gap at the floor line instead, running
  continuously and never interrupted by a door frame.

If a dimension in the drawings does not sit on a 300 multiple, it is a drafting
error, not a design decision. There are exactly three sanctioned exceptions: the
5,200 forecourt, the 1,200 pool terrace and the 205 mm stair riser.

---

## 2. Floors

| Location | Build-up | Finish |
|---|---|---|
| All ground-floor rooms | 40 mm travertine on 70 mm screed with UFH | Honed, unfilled, R10 |
| Terrace and pool deck | 40 mm travertine on pedestals | **Flamed**, R11 — same stone, different face |
| Bathrooms | Same travertine, 600 × 300 | Honed; falls formed in the screed, not the tile |
| First-floor bedrooms | Fumed oak, 180 mm board, 20 mm | Hardwax oil, matt |
| Stair treads | Fumed oak, 60 mm solid | Ends left square, no nosing |
| Plant room, dirty kitchen | Power-floated concrete, sealed | Falls to a gully |

The single most valuable move: **the same stone runs from the family living room
straight out onto the pool terrace at one level, with a 20 mm slot drain at the
threshold and no upstand.** The glazing sits *in* that slot. Inside and outside stop
being two places.

---

## 3. Walls

**Exposed concrete.** Board-marked using 150 mm sawn boards laid horizontally, the
board joint on the 300 grid so every second joint aligns with the stone. Tie holes
are set out deliberately at **900 × 900 centres**, recessed 20 mm and left open —
concrete that pretends it was not poured is worse than concrete that admits it.

- C32/40, **50 mm cover**, one supplier and one mix for the whole visible pour.
- **A sample panel 2.0 × 2.0 m must be cast, approved and kept on site** until the
  last visible pour is struck. This is not optional and it is the cheapest insurance
  on the project.
- No painting, no sealing beyond a matt penetrating anti-carbonation coat.

**Plastered walls.** Where concrete is not exposed: 3-coat gypsum, polished, in a
single off-white through the whole house. One colour. Not one per room.

**Stone-clad walls.** Boundary wall and the entrance flank: **40 mm** travertine on
stainless kerf clips, joints 6 mm dry, **rebated return stones at every external
corner** so no thin arris is ever visible.

---

## 4. Ceilings

Flush plaster with a **20 mm shadow gap** at every wall junction — the ceiling never
touches the wall. Services are coordinated to that gap: the linear diffuser, the
curtain pocket and the LED all live in it, and nothing else is allowed on the ceiling
plane except the lighting listed below.

Double-height void: exposed soffit, board-marked to match the walls, boards running
in the same direction as the walls below.

---

## 5. Openings

**Glazing.** Minimal-framed system with **18–22 mm sightlines**; anything heavier
stops reading as architect-specified. Panel capacity governs the layout: 6 m² per
panel in the standard range, 18 m² in the heavy range.

- Living room garden wall: **structural glass corner with no corner post.** This is
  the single most legible "architect" signal in the house and it should be protected
  in value engineering above almost anything else on this page.
- Glass: double-glazed, **SHGC 0.27, U 3.0 W/m²K**, low-e on face 2.
- Envelope targets, hot-dry: **WWR under 30 %**, wall **U 0.40**, roof **U 0.33**,
  thermal mass **≥ 230 mm**.

**Shading.** South openings get a horizontal projection of **0.6 × opening height**;
a 3.0 m opening therefore wants **1,800 mm**. East and west cannot be shaded by
horizontals at all — the west elevation uses **vertical fins at 600 mm centres,
450 × 180 mm**, which is the brise-soleil already in the model.

**Doors.** Full height, **2,700 mm**, trimless, no architrave, flush with the wall
plane, concealed hinges, magnetic catch. **Never a 2.1 m door under a 3.2 m ceiling
—** that one substitution undoes the whole scheme. Entrance pivot **1,400 × 2,900**,
bronze, with a full-height stainless pivot set flush in the stone.

**Ironmongery.** Bronze throughout, one family, lever on rose, no backplates.

---

## 6. Stair

Dog-leg, two flights, **20 risers at 205 mm**, going **250 mm**; 2R + G = **660 mm**,
inside the 550–700 comfort band. Clear width **1,200 mm**.

- Treads 60 mm solid fumed oak, ends square, **no nosing, no stringer**.
- **The supporting wall is RC, not blockwork** — tread stubs cast in, embedment
  **300 mm**. If this wall is value-engineered to blockwork the stair cannot be
  built as drawn, and that must be said out loud at tender.
- Balustrade: single 12 mm bronze rod at 1,000 mm, side-fixed, no posts.
- Under-tread LED in a 20 mm shadow gap, on the *wall* side only.

---

## 7. Lighting

Same rule as outside: **no visible source.** No downlights in any habitable room —
they are permitted only in the dirty kitchen, the plant room and the store.

| Room | Method |
|---|---|
| Entrance hall, void | Concealed cove in the ceiling shadow gap, grazing the concrete |
| Living, majlis, dining | Wall-washing from a slot at the ceiling perimeter + table lamps on 5 A circuits |
| Kitchen | Under-cabinet linear at the front edge of the wall unit, never the back |
| Bedrooms | Bedside pendants on 5 A; cove to the ceiling; nothing overhead |
| Bathrooms | Vertical strips **either side** of the mirror, never above it |
| Stair | Under-tread only |

3000 K everywhere, **CRI ≥ 90**, all dimmable on DALI. Four scenes; the switch plates
are bronze, two-gang maximum, and every plate in the house sits at **1,050 mm** to
centre — including the ones nobody will look at.

---

## 8. The things that get value-engineered, and what happens

Written down now so the conversation later is short:

| Proposal | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Blockwork instead of RC at the stair | The stair cannot be built. Redesign, don't substitute. |
| Standard 2.1 m doors | The proportion system fails in every room at once. |
| Framed glazing at the living corner | The one unmistakable architectural move is gone. |
| Filled travertine | Cheaper, and it looks it — the unfilled face is the whole reason for the stone. |
| Skimmer pool instead of deck level | You have bought a normal pool. The freeboard is the point. |
| Mixed colour temperature | Free to avoid, and the most visible fault a visitor will notice. |
| Dropping the sample concrete panel | Saves ~£2k, risks the entire exposed-concrete package. |

---

## 9. Fixtures and fittings actually drawn

49 blocks are placed in the plans, all real ACA content, none of them symbols. Every
one is checked by `villa_furniture_spec.py` against three rules — inside its room, no
overlap with another item, and clear of every door swing — plus a clearance rule:
**550 mm** in front of a WC or basin, **700 mm** to one side of a bath or bed. That
check currently returns **0 issues**, and it is the check that found the two en-suites
that were too small to hold a bath at all.

Every one of the 49 was then measured back **out of AutoCAD** and compared with the
footprint the plan demands: 21/21 on the ground floor and 28/28 on the first. Blocks
are scaled uniformly and never distorted to fit, so a block whose aspect differs
slightly from the catalogue lands a few millimetres short on one axis — placement is
therefore held exact and size is allowed that residual. The one block that failed
turned out to have a wrong catalogue entry (the fridge, measured with its door swing
included), which was corrected at source rather than tolerated.

constraint, not an omission.
