# Villa design intelligence — 697 m² plot, 418 m² GFA

Research completed 2026-09-06. Sources at the end. **Two caveats from the
researcher:** setback/coverage/wall-height figures are expected ranges, not a code
citation for this jurisdiction — verify with the authority. The vanishing-edge
flow-rate-per-metre figure is a trade rule of thumb, not a standard.

---

## THE THREE FINDINGS THAT CHANGE THE DESIGN

### 1. A classic infinity edge WILL NOT WORK on this plot
A vanishing edge only reads if the viewer's eye is above the weir **and there is
something to spill toward** — general minimum a **1.2–1.8 m drop from pool rim to
the terrain beyond**. On a flat 697 m² plot enclosed by a stone wall, the edge
spills toward a wall 3–6 m away and reads as nothing. Two honest options:

- **(a) Perimeter / knife-edge overflow** — water dead level with the deck on all
  four sides, mirror-flat. Needs no fall. The correct answer for a flat walled
  site. This is the Lautner detail.
- **(b) Raised pool with a designed one-sided overflow** — lift the pool
  **600–900 mm** above the terrace so the spill face becomes a waterwall into a
  visible basin. Also solves the boundary-wall 45° structural problem.

Cost: infinity edge +20–40% over a plain pool; knife-edge perimeter overflow can
approach the cost of the rest of the pool again.

### 2. The programme does not fit in 418 m²
A full luxury schedule sums to **480–620 m² net before circulation — 20–45% more
than the brief**. Resolve by one of: (a) push majlis, garage, plant, staff and pool
plant into a **semi-basement** (the F.LOT strategy), (b) 4 bedrooms not 5, (c) take
the mid-range of every number. **Do not attempt all three at the top.**

### 3. The Alhambra proportion fits this plot almost exactly
**Court of the Myrtles: courtyard 23.5 × 36.6 m, pool 34.0 × 7.10 m** — 93% of the
court's length, 30% of its width, 4.8:1. This plot is 24 × 29 m, almost exactly the
Myrtles' width. **A 20–24 m × 4.5–5.0 m axial water body on the long dimension is a
historically proven proportion.** (Earlier scheme had 12 × 3.5 m running the short
way — weaker.)

### THE SECTION WORTH STEALING (researcher's own synthesis)
Put the servant zone (garage, staff, plant, pool plant, and the majlis/dewaniya if
separated) in a **semi-basement lit by a sunken courtyard**; **float the pool over
part of it**; **raise the ground floor half a level** above the street; carry a
**double-height volume through both upper storeys on the garden side**;
**cantilever the first floor 2.5–3.0 m over the pool**; terminate in a **shaded roof
terrace**. F.LOT + Interlude House combined — the right answer for 697 m².

## 1. PRECEDENTS

**F.LOT — Studio Toggle, Al Bida'a, Kuwait, 2016 — site 750 m² (closest match).**
Two floating masses intersecting at right angles; **5 m cantilever**. A **15 m pool
floating above the parking**, bisecting the longitudinal volume; ground floor open
plan on *both sides* of the pool, joined by a **bridge encased in sliding glass**.
Basement holds the *dewaniya* (men's majlis), **3-car parking directly under the
pool**, and staff, all lit by a **sunken courtyard finished in white pebbles**.
→ On 750 m² the entire servant zone goes underground and the pool becomes the garage
roof. The pebble court is what makes the basement a served space, not a cellar.

**Interlude House — AAd | Ayutt and Associates, Bangkok, 2021 — site 800 m², built 950 m².**
Ground floor an **opaque black box to the street**; entry through a black portal into
a **cave-like garage lit by clerestory**. Mass on three sides; pool + courtyard at the
heart, south. **House raised half a level** so exterior sightlines strike the ceiling,
not the occupants — cheapest privacy device available. Living room read *across the
courtyard* through a double-height glass wall. **Upper floor cantilevers over ~⅓ of
the pool.** Floating stone stair under a clerestory acting as a sundial; recessed
glazing plane forming a continuous balcony; external shutters with aluminium fins.
→ Maps almost one-to-one onto this brief.

**Fish House — Guz Architects, Singapore, 2009 — site 726 m², GFA 540 m².**
**Basement media room with a U-shaped acrylic window into the pool** — the pool is the
basement's light source and its view. Curved roofs with thin-film PV and green roof.
→ 726/540 is essentially this ratio (697/418). Proof the brief is buildable at quality.

**Mop House — AGi Architects, Kuwait, 2010 — ~1,300 m².**
Two interlocked volumes forming an open-air courtyard, organised diagonally about a
central axis. **Two street entrances — one public, one private**, with circulation
and lift placed so the house can later split into two dwellings. **The pool sits in
the main public living area and is revealed the instant you enter.** First floor
overhangs to shade the ground floor.
→ Dual-entrance discipline expressed in the *massing*, not just the plan.

**Casa Mérida — Ludwig Godefroy, Mexico, 2018 — plot 8 × 80 m (640 m²), built 250 m².**
One long concrete wall is structure, circulation spine and visual axis (*sacbé*).
**An 80 m uninterrupted perspective from the entrance door to the pool**, with a
perennial water channel from the entry. Inverts local norm: service to the street,
living/pool at the back. Cream Mayan stone perimeter wall.
→ The single most powerful move on a small plot is one long uninterrupted axis with
water on it, terminating in the pool.

**AB House — Pitsou Kedem, Israel, 2016 — house 770 m².**
Long exposed-concrete box **floating above an excavated yard and a reflecting pool**,
plus a taller rendered perpendicular mass. Sunken courtyard garden; white perforated
metal grid screens.
→ Excavating a sunken court and floating a box over it buys section drama on a flat site.

**Vienna Way — Marmol Radziner, Venice CA — lot 19.8 × 53.3 m.**
**Divides the lot into thirds.** Two volumes on the outer edges, middle third is the
outdoor room, bridged by a **sunken kitchen overlooking the pool**. South volume
single-storey, north volume two-storey with its own courtyard.
→ "Thirds" is a legible parti; the pool is the designed void *between* masses.

**Vertical Courtyard House — Montalba, Santa Monica — 506 m², three storeys + basement.**
Organised around a **three-storey atrium** pulling light to the basement. L-plan;
bridge carries circulation. Pool sited to create an **evaporative cool-air corridor**.
→ On a tight plot the light-well *is* the luxury.

**Wall House — AGi, Kuwait, 2015.** Monumental stone wall to the street; **four patios
developed three-dimensionally, four sheltered roof terraces**; floor voids linking
storeys; parents low / communal middle / daughters top.

## 2. THE MOVES THAT SIGNAL QUALITY

**Threshold compression / release.** Compress at **2.1–2.4 m soffit, 1.5–2.0 m wide**,
low light; release into **5.5–6.5 m**. Ratio ≥ **1:2.2** for the release to register.
Wright's entries ~1.98–2.13 m. Do it twice: gate → forecourt → entry loggia → hall → volume.

**Double-height volume.** **2.7 m = minimum compliance; 3.0–3.2 m = luxury begins;
3.3 m+ or 4.5–6.0 m double-height = ultra-luxury.** With 400–600 mm slab+services,
floor-to-floor 3.6–3.9 m → two-storey void **6.0–7.0 m clear**. Plan it at least as
wide as it is tall in one direction; below ~5 × 5 m it reads as a shaft. Best
practice: **~60% of the living room single-height at 3.0–3.2 m (the sofa zone), 40%
double-height at the glazed garden edge.**

**Cantilever.** Eurocode 2 span/effective-depth basic **8**, to ~**14** with steel-stress
modification; deflection span/250 appearance, span/500 good performance (EN 1992-1-1
cl 7.4.1(4)). 3 m RC cantilever → ~215–375 mm effective depth; 3.5–4 m → 300–450 mm;
beyond ~4.5 m go post-tensioned or steel. **2.5–3.5 m is the villa sweet spot.**
Detail the soffit with a drip and a **20 mm shadow gap** at the wall junction.

**Brise-soleil.** South overhang **projection = 0.4 × opening height at 8–20°N, 0.5 ×
at 20–25°N, 0.6–0.8 × at 25°N+**. At Dubai latitude a 3.0 m opening wants
**1.8–2.4 m**. East/west **cannot** be shaded by horizontals — vertical fins, deep
recesses or operable louvres. Fixed horizontal louvre spacing ≈ **75% of blade width**.
Size with **VSA = arctan(projection ÷ window height)**. Hot-dry envelope (ECBC 2017):
WWR **<30%**, SHGC **0.27**, glazing U **3.0**, wall U **0.40**, roof U **0.33 W/m²K**,
thermal mass **230 mm** min.

**Courtyard.** Hot-dry **height/width ≈ 1.5** for summer shade. Aspect **1:1 to 2:1**
minimises discomfort; **1:1 to 1:1.5** floor-to-wall for daylight without glare. On a
two-storey villa (7–8 m to parapet) a **5–7 m wide** court gives H/W ≈ 1.1–1.6.
Minimum useful court for a tree: **4 × 4 m with 1.0–1.2 m soil depth**.

**Axial water / reflecting pool.** Depth **50–150 mm; standard 75 mm over a
black-finished slab.** Darkness and stillness make the mirror, not depth. Turnover
every **2–4 hours**, submerged low-velocity inlets, no visible jets, no audible
splash. **Automatic float valve essential** — a mirror that drops 5 mm reads as
broken. **Rill width 300–600 mm.** Geometry rule: *for an element to read fully in
reflection, the pool must extend in the viewing direction by at least the apparent
height of that element from the viewing point.*

**Floating stair.** Going ≥ **280 mm** (luxury 300–320); riser **100–180** (luxury 170);
tread **64–114 mm** (stone/timber cantilevers 80–100); nosing 25–38; max 30°. Steel
plate cores ~**10 mm at ~500 mm cantilever**. Width: specify **1,100–1,400 mm**.
**Supporting wall must be RC, not blockwork**, tread stubs cast in, embedment
250–350 mm. No stringer, no visible fixings, glass or single-rod balustrade.

**Clerestory.** A side window lights to **1.5–2 ×** head height; a clerestory at 3 m or
on a double-height wall pushes daylight **4–6 m** back — a **2.5 × head-height** rule.
Sill above **2.1 m**; band **450–750 mm**. Use it where you have a blank boundary
wall: the top 600 mm of a 7 m elevation sees sky, not the neighbour. Ventilation inlet
area ≥ **1/10 of floor area** for stack effect.

**Framed views.** On a walled urban plot you have no view — manufacture one. Seated eye
level **1,067–1,150 mm**; for a seated view set the sill at **300–450 mm**. Minimal
glazing sightlines **18–22 mm** read as architect-specified (Vitrocsa 18 mm interlocks,
22 mm vertical). Panel capacity **6 m²** (3001 range, 26 mm DGU) up to **18 m²** (TH+);
turnable corner panels 6 m²/250 kg. **A structural glass corner with no corner post is
the single most legible "architect" signal in a villa.**

**Detail discipline.** Shadow gaps **10–20 mm deep × 10–20 mm tall** (20 mm takes an
LED strip). Trimless door openings, no architraves. **Full-height doors 2.7–3.0 m** —
never a 2.1 m door under a 3.2 m ceiling. Pivot entrance **1,200–1,500 wide ×
2,700–3,000 high**. Stone **30 mm min limestone/marble, 20 mm dense granite**;
mechanically fixed kerf systems take 30/40 mm panels on stainless clips. **One
setting-out grid** — stone joint, ceiling joint, door head, window head and skirting
all on the same module. That discipline is what reads as "expensive".

## 3. INFINITY / OVERFLOW POOL — TECHNICAL

**Dimensions.** For 697 m² plot / 418 m² house: **10–14 m × 3.5–4.5 m; target
12.0 × 4.0 m = 48 m²**. Lap swim ≥12 m; below 8 m is a plunge pool. Volume at 1.4 m
avg ≈ **67 m³**. Water surface ~**10% of plot** — with ~490 m² open, 45–50 m² is
right; above ~60 m² the garden disappears.

**Deck-level vs skimmer.** Skimmer: water 100–150 mm below coping, visible tile band,
one skimmer per 25–30 m². Always reads domestic. **Deck-level: water level with the
deck, spilling into a slot — the absence of freeboard is the whole point.**
- **Slot width 19–25 mm.**
- **Gutter min 75 × 75 mm**, lip ≤ ~300 mm below deck, fall **1:48**.
- **Drop pipes 75–100 mm dia at ~1.5 m centres**; vents 38 mm.
- **Bond beam 500–610 mm wide**, extending ~600 mm down.
- **Edge tile** 9.5 mm granite, 300 × 300, **bevelled leading edge**, laid falling away.
- **LEVEL TOLERANCE ±0.8 mm OVER THE WHOLE PERIMETER.** This number decides whether
  the pool works. Set with a laser during the pour and again at tiling.

**Weir wall / lip (vanishing-edge version).** Crest sits **2–6 mm below the required
water level** — that differential, not the pump, sets sheet thickness. Top of wall
dense machined granite **20–40 mm**, knife edge or **3–6 mm radius** on the leading
arris. **RC wall, 200–250 mm at the crest**, widening at base. A **25 mm error**
destroys the effect. Flow **40–60 L/min per linear metre** thin sheet, **90–150** heavy
curtain *(trade rule of thumb — confirm with the pool engineer)*. **The vanishing-edge
pump is always separate from filtration.** Catch basin water level **150–300 mm below
the crest** so return water is neither seen nor heard.

**Catch basin / balance tank.** Most usable rule: **1 US gal per ft² of surface =
40.7 L/m²**. For 48 m² → **≈1,950 L**. Trade range for residential: **1,900–5,700 L**,
i.e. roughly **3–8% of pool volume**. Build it up honestly instead:
bather displacement **60–80 L/bather** (8–10 people = 500–800 L) + water in transit
(32 m gutter → **400–800 L**) + start/stop surge + backwash **500–1,500 L** +
freeboard **150–250 mm** + low-level cut-out **150–200 mm**. Take the larger of that
and 40.7 L/m². Physical size for 2,500–3,500 L ≈ **2.0 × 1.2 × 1.6 m deep**.
**Tank invert 1.0–1.5 m below pool water line** for gravity fill and flooded suction.
**The catch basin can be the balance tank** if sized, accessible, ventilated and
level-controlled — on a small plot that is the right move, one chamber not two.
Autofill solenoid + float **on the tank, not the pool**. Evaporation **5–10 mm/day**
hot-dry = **240–480 L/day** for 48 m².

**Freeboard / coping.** Skimmer 100–150 mm; deck-level **zero**. Coping **30–40 mm**
thick, **20–30 mm overhang**, **6–8 mm drip groove** set 15–20 mm back. High-end
reveal: **10–15 mm shadow gap** between coping and deck, or flush coping with a 6 mm
dry joint and no visible mortar.

**Plant room.** **12–18 m² clear, 2.4 m clear height** (2.1 absolute min), plus balance
chamber if not internal. Fits: sand/glass filter (0.9–1.2 m dia, 1.6 m tall),
filtration pump, **separate overflow pump**, heat pump/exchanger, pH + chlorine dosing
bunded, UV/ozone, panel, manifolds. **600–800 mm clearance around the filter, 1.0 m in
front of the panel.** Keep the pipe run **under 15 m**. Floor **1.0–1.5 m below pool
water level**. Needs a **floor gully (the room will flood)**, a **1.0 m clear door**,
mechanical ventilation, and **acoustic separation — never share a wall with a bedroom
or the majlis.** Heat pump needs outdoor air.

**Structural / site.** **The 45° load-spread rule governs:** the pool shell must not
intrude into a plane drawn at 45° from the bottom outside edge of the adjacent
footing. A 1.5 m deep pool beside a boundary-wall footing at 0.6 m wants
**0.9–1.5 m clear ground**, or the pool wall must be designed as a retaining wall
carrying the wall's surcharge. Zoning setback typically **1.5 m from a property line**
(verify). **Above a basement:** water-retaining structure on a suspended slab —
**crack width ≤ 0.2 mm**, full tanking, **drained cavity with leak detection**,
movement/isolation detail at the perimeter. **Never rely on tile adhesive as
waterproofing.** Shell RC **250–300 mm**, C32/40 with waterproofing admixture,
**50 mm cover** to the earth face.

**Depths.** **1.2 m shallow to 1.6–1.8 m deep, or 1.4 m constant** (increasingly the
choice for overflow pools — simpler hydraulics and shell, suits adults). Under 2.0 m
unless diving. **Slope max 1:12 in water shallower than 1.5 m**, no steeper than 1:3
beyond; break of slope at the deep end. **Sun/Baja shelf: 250–350 mm deep, 1.8–2.5 m
front-to-back, 2.5–4.0 m along one end** — doubles as mirror surface and lounging
zone, best value-for-money element in a luxury pool. **Steps 300 rise / 300 going**,
top step at deck level.

## 4. SITE PLANNING NUMBERS

*Ranges to expect, then verify with the authority.*

**Setbacks ~700 m² villa plot.** Gulf: front **3.0–6.0 m** (5 m common G+1), sides
**3.0 m**, rear **3.0 m**; some allow a reduced side 1.5–2.0 m or zero-lot on a corner.
Spain/Balearics: **3.0 m** boundaries, **5.0 m** street, *ocupación* **30%*,
*edificabilidad* 0.4–0.6. California: front **6.1–7.6 m**, side **1.5–3.0 m**, rear
**4.6–7.6 m**; R1 FAR 0.45–0.50, coverage 35–45%.

**Arithmetic for 24 × 29 m.** 5/3/3 setbacks → buildable **18 × 21 m = 378 m²**.
418 m² over two storeys + roof → footprint **~190–220 m² = 27–32% coverage**,
comfortably inside most limits. Ground 200–220, first 170–190, roof enclosure 30–45.
**BUA ratio 418/697 = 0.60.** Check whether the authority counts basement, garage,
roof enclosure, covered terraces. **In Dubai a lightweight pergola is excluded from
BUA** — a real planning lever (over 20 m² or 3 m high needs a permit, design for
**1 kN/m²** wind). Open ground left ~**480–500 m²**: pool+deck 100–120, arrival court
+ drive 90–110, main garden 150–200, side service strips 60–80.

**Boundary walls / gates.** Gulf: solid to **2.4 m** side/rear, **1.8–2.0 m** front,
sometimes top 600 mm required open; some cap front at 1.2–1.5 m solid + screen.
US: **1.83 m** side/rear, **0.9–1.2 m** in the front setback. Spain 1.0–1.2 m solid +
screen to 2.0–2.2 m. **Stone wall 300–400 mm overall**, or blockwork core with
**30–40 mm** stone both sides; piers at **3.0–4.0 m** centres, movement joint every
**6–8 m**, **100–150 mm coping with a drip**. To read as *solid* stone, use
**100–150 mm stone with a rebated return** so no thin edge is visible.
**Vehicle gate 3.5 m clear min, 4.0–4.5 m comfortable**; cantilever slider needs
run-back = opening + 500 mm. **Pedestrian gate 1.0–1.2 m.** **Standing space
5.5–6.0 m deep** between gate line and street.

**Driveway / garage.** Driveway **3.0 m min one-way, 3.5–4.0 comfortable**, 5.5–6.0
two-way. Turning: car **5.5–6.0 m** kerb radius, SUV **6.5–7.0**; turning to exit
forwards needs a **13–15 m circle** (not available here) or a hammerhead —
**6.0 × 3.0 m bay off a 5.5 m aisle**, or a **6.0 × 12.0 m forecourt**. Realistically
accept reversing and shape the apron for one straight movement.
**Garage 2 cars: min 5.5 × 5.5 (30 m²); comfortable 6.0 × 6.5 (39 m²); luxury
6.5–7.0 wide × 7.0 deep (46–49 m²).** Per bay 2.7–3.0 m, **900 mm between cars,
600 mm to walls**, clear height **2.4 m min, 2.7 m** if a lift may be added.
**Two single 2.7 m doors beat one 5.0–5.5 m double.** Aisle in front **6.0 m min**.
**Basement ramp 1:8 comfortable, 1:6 max**, transitions at half gradient for
2.0–2.5 m. A 3.0 m drop at 1:6 needs **18 m of run plus transitions** — usually
impossible on a 29 m plot, which is exactly why F.LOT puts the garage under the pool
and why a **car lift (pit ~3.0 × 6.0 m)** becomes the alternative.

**Terraces / pool decks.** Circulation only **1.5–1.8 m**; lounge terrace
**3.5–4.5 m**; dining for 8–10 **4.5–5.0 deep × 5.0–6.0 wide**. Lounger +
circulation **3.0 m min, 3.5 comfortable**; two facing rows + path **6.0 m**.
**Pool surround: 1.2 m min on back sides, 1.5 m where people walk, 3.5–4.5 m on the
lounging side.** A 600 mm coping walk is a developer's pool. Terrace falls **1.5–2%**;
flat roof decks **1:80 min**.

- **Roof ≈33 enclosed:** stair head + lift overrun 16 · roof pantry/WC 8 · store 9 ·

its own WC and must not open into the kitchen or a guest corridor. **The service route
must reach the majlis for coffee without crossing family living** — on a small plot
that means a short service corridor behind the show kitchen with a discreet door into
the majlis.

## 6. LEVEL CHANGES AND SECTION

- **Split level:** offset = half the floor-to-floor — **1.75–1.95 m**, stair of 10–11
  risers. Below ~1.2 m a split reads as a mistake.
- **Cheapest privacy device:** raise the ground floor half a level. Even **600–900 mm**
  means a passer-by's sightline through the glazing hits your ceiling, not your sofa.
- **Sunken lounge:** 2–3 risers at 150–180, or one 200 mm step; total drop
  **300–500 mm** (comfortable range 150–350; beyond ~350 an accessibility problem).
  Seating area **at least 3.6 × 3.6 m**. No nosing, no handrail, floor material turning
  down the riser, linear light or shadow gap at the riser.
- **External steps:** rise **100–150** (120–140 sweet spot), going **300–400**,
  **2R + G = 550–700** (600–650 ideal). Never a single step in an open terrace.
- **Raised pool deck:** lifting the pool **450–900 mm** gives (a) a seat-height edge at
  450, (b) somewhere to hide the balance tank and gutter, (c) a spill face that can be
  a real waterwall, (d) clearance from the 45° zone of the boundary-wall footing.
  **This is the move that makes an "infinity" edge possible on a flat plot.**
- **Sunken courtyard:** excavate **2.7–3.3 m**; min **4 × 4 m**, ideally **5–6 m**
  facing the glazing. Finish light (white pebble) to bounce diffused light in; pumped
  sump for a 1-in-100-year storm; glaze the basement wall full height.
- **Roof terrace:** guarding **min 1,100 mm**. Waterproofing turned up **min 150 mm**
  above paving, mechanically fixed and counter-flashed; at a level-access threshold the
  upstand may drop to **75 mm** with a recessed linear channel and the membrane
  returning to 150 mm adjacent. Deck fall **1:80 min**; provide an overflow because the
  parapet encloses the area. Pedestal build-up **150–250 mm**.
- **Roof pergola:** **2.7–3.0 m clear** under beams. Aluminium is the Gulf default.
  Decking surface temperatures **65–70 °C** in afternoon sun — governs material choice.
